## Where both sides agreed

Grounded in the WHO *Healthy diet* text (with line citations from the fetched source):

- WHO expressly supports **both instruments in dispute**: "establishing taxes" on products high in free sugars, and "implementing **mandatory** instruments to protect children from the harmful impact of food marketing."
- WHO frames sugar in a **moderation** model, not a zero-intake one: free sugars "less than 10%" of daily energy (ideally "5% or less"); even fruit juice "can be consumed," though its free sugars "should be limited."
- The strongest beverage-specific avoidance language is **narrow**: for "infants and young children of 6–23 months of age," foods high in sugar and "sweet beverages should be avoided."
- WHO prescribes a **mixed toolkit**, not a tax in isolation: subsidies, reformulation, affordable healthy options, portion-size and pricing review, school/workplace standards, point-of-sale information, interpretive front-of-pack labelling, education, and dietary counselling.
- Reductions must be achieved **without non-sugar sweeteners** — no "swap the chemistry" loophole.
- **Affordability/regressivity** is part of the problem WHO itself names ("income, food prices"), so taxes must be paired with subsidies.

## Where they stayed split (why it's *partial*)

- **Degree.** Does WHO's toolkit justify treatment *"comparable to tobacco"* across the whole category (CON: no), or strong-but-distinct nutrition policy?
- **"Punitive."** WHO endorses taxes but specifies no rate, no punishment principle, and no tobacco analogue — so "punitive" is not established by the source.
- **Adult marketing bans.** WHO is explicit only about *children*; blanket adult-facing ad bans are a policy judgment beyond the text.
- **The breast-milk-substitutes precedent.** Evidence that strict marketing law can apply beyond tobacco-like products, or a special infant-feeding case?

## The shared recommended decision

Adopt the instruments WHO clearly supports, but do **not** claim full tobacco parity from this source alone:

1. **Sugar-tiered excise** on sugar-sweetened drinks, banded by sugar content, with compliance judged by *lower sugar exposure* — not by switching to non-sugar sweeteners.
2. **Mandatory marketing restrictions in child-directed media, schools, and similar settings**, where WHO's language is explicit. Extensions beyond children should be labelled a policy choice beyond the source.
3. **Pair the tax with WHO's structural measures**: subsidies for healthier foods, school/workplace availability standards, reformulation targets, and portion-size/pricing review.
4. **For adults, prefer information/environment tools over a tobacco-style ban**: point-of-sale information, interpretive front-of-pack labelling, education, and dietary counselling.
5. **Review against WHO's own endpoints** — lower free-sugar intake toward "<10%" and ideally "5% or less," lower product sugar content, and no worsening of the affordability constraints WHO itself identifies.
